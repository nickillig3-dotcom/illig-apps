Past Sprints, Sprint History & Scope Change

Documentation for the Jira Cloud dashboard gadgets by Illig Apps

Install

A Jira administrator installs the app from the Atlassian Marketplace. There is nothing to configure afterwards and no account to create: the app runs on Atlassian’s own infrastructure and reads your issues through Jira’s API as the person looking at the gadget.

Add a gadget to a dashboard

  1. Open Dashboards and pick the dashboard you want, or create one.
  2. Choose Add gadget, search for Sprint, and add Sprint History or Sprint Scope Change.
  3. The gadget opens its settings straight away. Fill them in and choose Save.

To change the settings later, use the gadget’s … menu and choose Edit, or switch the whole dashboard to edit mode.

Settings

Project
The project whose sprints you want to report on. Sprints are found through the issues in your selection, so this decides both which sprints appear and which issues are counted.
Saved filter
One of your favourite filters, instead of a whole project. Overrides the project above.
JQL
Any JQL query, for example project = ABC AND type != Sub-task. Overrides both of the settings above. Use this when a board’s scope is narrower than its project.
Sprint (Sprint Scope Change only)
Which sprint to break down. The default, Most recently completed sprint, follows your team automatically and needs no maintenance.
How far back (Sprint History only)
How many sprints to show, newest ones last. Between 3 and 30.
What to measure
Story point estimate, original estimate, remaining estimate, any other numeric field on your site, or Issue count. The list is read from your own instance, so it contains your custom fields under the names your site uses. Story points are offered first.
Issues read at most
An upper bound on how many issues the gadget reads, between 100 and 8000. If the bound is reached the gadget says so instead of showing a smaller number as if it were complete.
List the individual issues (Sprint Scope Change only)
Show each issue with its bucket and the status it held when the sprint ended.

What the numbers mean

CommittedIn the sprint at the moment it was started.
AddedPut into the sprint after it had started. This is the scope creep.
RemovedTaken out of the sprint before it ended.
Added and removed againPut in and taken out again inside the same sprint.
DeliveredIn a status of the Done category when the sprint ended — not the status the issue has today.
Carried overStill in the sprint when it ended, and not delivered.
Scope change(added − removed) ÷ committed, as a percentage. A sprint with nothing committed shows no percentage rather than dividing by zero.

An issue that was removed from the sprint and finished afterwards is deliberately not counted as that sprint’s output. Counting it there is the easiest way to make a velocity figure flatter than the team actually was.

Where the numbers come from

Jira can say which issues are in a sprint now. It cannot say which were in it when the sprint started, and an issue that was pulled out leaves no trace on the sprint at all. So the app reconstructs it from the issue changelog:

  1. Every change to the Sprint field writes a changelog entry, and each entry carries the complete new list of sprints. Sorted by time these form a series of steps, and the membership at any instant is the last step at or before it.
  2. The membership is read at the sprint’s start and at its completion. The two answers give the four buckets above.
  3. The same replay is done on the status field to find the status each issue held when the sprint ended.
This is the calculation behind JRACLOUD-75868, open since 2016 with over 1,400 votes, which asks Jira for exactly this and has not been scheduled.

The three cases where the gadget says it is not sure

“Only the first N issues were read”
The selection is larger than the issue limit, so sprints further back may be incomplete. Narrow the filter, show fewer sprints, or raise the limit.
“The list of done statuses could not be read”
Jira did not return the status catalogue, so nothing can be counted as delivered. Every other figure is still correct.
“N issues had no recorded status when the sprint ended”
Those issues are counted as not delivered. This happens when an issue’s history was trimmed or imported without one.

Limits worth knowing before you buy

  • A sprint that no longer contains a single issue cannot be found and does not appear.
  • Sprints are found through the issues in your selection, so an issue moved to another project after a sprint is no longer counted in it.
  • Future sprints are not shown: they have no start date and nothing to report.
  • Statuses are compared by their id, so renaming a status does not break the history. A status that is deleted outright cannot be classified afterwards.

Permissions and data

The app asks for a single scope, read:jira-work, and never writes to your issues. Every request runs as the person looking at the gadget, so a viewer sees only the issues their own Jira permissions allow. The app stores nothing, runs entirely on Atlassian infrastructure and makes no request to any server outside Atlassian.
